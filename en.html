STORY 01 | The promise

"Free electricity, forever."

Limitless sunlight and low-power LEDs: together, they promised freedom from electricity bills. Billed as "the ultimate streetlight," solar streetlights drew in everyone from major corporations to small local electrical contractors.

Solar power + LED = zero electricity cost.
Everyone believed it.

STORY 02 | The reality

Three days of rain,
and the light went out.

A solar streetlight lights the night only with the power it stored in its battery during the day. When cloud and rain go on, it can't generate enough, and the reserve soon runs out. Rainy seasons, long autumn rains, typhoons: under Japan's skies, solar streetlights that went dark were all too common.

Rain, day 1On
Rain, day 2Dimmed
Rain, day 3Off

The light needed most in a disaster went out exactly when it mattered most.

STORY 03 | The idea

What if the light it shines
could be used again?

A streetlight gives off light all night long. What if its solar panels could catch that light and turn it back into electricity? Solar Loop turns this overlooked idea into working technology.

It generates power from the sun by day and from its own light by night. Because the light circulates, it holds up through long rains and disasters.

Developed and patented by Kume Denki Co., Ltd. (US Patent 8,853,947 B2) Planning and sales: Lightplan Co., Ltd.

MECHANISM

Four steps of circulating light

  1. 01 Daytime generation The solar panels generate power from sunlight.
  2. 02 Battery storage The power is stored in the battery.
  3. 03 Sensor-activated lighting When it gets dark, a sensor switches the LED on.
  4. 04 Power from its own light The panels catch the LED's light at night and generate power again. That is the Solar Loop.

PROOF | One year in the field

Four seasons.
Never once went dark.

From the Osaka waterfront to winter in Aichi. At two sites in different seasons, it stayed lit every night for about a year under Japan's weather.

April–October 2025 | Spring, rainy season, summer, typhoons

Expo 2025 Osaka, Kansai

Installed in the Water Terminal area. For all 184 days of the Expo, it lit every night on solar power alone, with no grid power.

November 2025–April 2026 | Autumn, winter, early spring

Toyohashi Zoo and Botanical Park

Winter brings short days and long nights, the toughest conditions for a solar streetlight. Even then, the light never went out.
